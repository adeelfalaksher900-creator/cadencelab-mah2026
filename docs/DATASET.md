# Dataset card

Version 1: five reference excerpts, 90 paired cases; Barack Obama (two weekly addresses, 2017-01-07 and 2017-01-14) and John F. Kennedy (1961 inaugural excerpt). Two speakers and three speeches do not establish demographic coverage.

## Sources and rights

- Obama audio: official archived White House MP3s; video/source pages identify this presidential material as public domain. Transcripts: `https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/2017/01/07/weekly-address-president-obamas-farewell-address-nation` and `https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/2017/01/14/weekly-address-honor-serving-you-president`.
- JFK excerpt: `Xenova/transformers.js-docs` `jfk.wav`, pinned at `159e0fd398b57d12a34e6a01e426e9e416ffce30`, a short excerpt of the U.S. presidential inaugural recording. Speech and recording provenance: `https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/president-john-f-kennedys-inaugural-address` and `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JFK_inaugural_address.ogg`. The latter marks the U.S. federal-government recording public domain. The mirror is credited as the actual byte-download source; we do not claim a direct Commons download succeeded.
- No affiliation, endorsement, current political recommendation or human performance rating is implied. Local laws concerning public-domain works can differ. Historical archival channels differ in noise and production quality.
- Unused FDR candidates and 1920s recordings were excluded; their redistribution rights and acoustic cleanliness were not established sufficiently.

## Construction

Download the source recordings and exact transcripts. Align the full official Obama transcript to the full speech before cropping excerpts; using a transcript prefix against an entire untrimmed recording causes invalid timings. Normalize English spelling and write numbers as words. Resample to mono 16 kHz. Store FLAC and re-read the serialized waveform before feature analysis.

For each reference, inject one local property over an aligned word window. Ground truth records the transformation's exact sample interval, independently of detector output. Changes are not generated by editing detector timestamps. Tempo uses FFmpeg `atempo`, pitch uses `rubberband`, energy multiplies samples, pauses insert zeros, and seeded Gaussian noise uses recorded SNR values.

| Transformation | Subtle | Moderate | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local pace | 1.15x | 1.40x | 1.80x |
| Local gain | 0.50 | 0.25 | 0.10 |
| Added pause | 0.4 s | 0.9 s | 1.8 s |
| White-noise SNR | 20 dB | 10 dB | 0 dB |
| Local pitch | +2 st | +4 st | +7 st |

Three controls per reference: unchanged, whole-recording gain 0.5, whole-recording pitch +3 semitones. Every source has the same transformation family. Strict controls can reveal alignment and pitch-estimation artifacts; they are not removed to improve headline scores.

## File schema

Each case JSON contains `reference_id`, `audio`, shared `transcript`, `ground_truth`, `words`, frame `features`, `events`, per-word evidence, `rubric`, alignment confidence, transcript-support error and status. SHA-256 binds each serialized audio file. `references.json` includes the original interval and source attribution. Scores are withheld for abstained cases.

## Intended use and limits

Controlled regression tests of acoustic-change localization, transparent rubric analysis and UI demonstrations. Not a training corpus for grading people; not a test of persuasive content, truth, emotions, intelligence, diagnosis or human judging reliability. Thresholds were developed on this corpus, so all numbers are development diagnostics. There is no held-out split or validated student outcome claim. The synthetic noise and pitch transforms can introduce artifacts different from actual speaking errors.
